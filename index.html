About Me
I am a PhD candidate in human geography at the University of Washington. I study political and cultural geographies with a focuses on local democracy, trust, elections, liberalism, and political philosophy.
Originally from New Hampshire, I moved to Minnesota to attend Macalester College, California to earn my teaching degree, and then moved back to New Hampshire where I taught high school social studies.
My hobbies include running and reading fiction.
I live in Seattle with my wife, Jennifer, and our two cats, Yucca and Chowder.

My dissertation, "After Much Discussion": Participation, Trust, and Boredom in the New England Town Meeting, examines the institution of New England Town Meeting — a form of direct, participatory, deliberative, actually existing local democracy. Observers often expect to find contestation and disagreement, but my research indicates that the opposite is more likely — issues are largely decided through deliberation, consensus, and even disengaged apathy. I examine town meeting through a self-created longitudinal dataset of over 8,000 warrant articles (1956–2025) from over 200 meetings. In addition, I draw on ethnographic observation of 20 town meetings (2025–26), which I was able to observe through funded fieldwork. My dissertation asks us to reconsider low rates of participation not as failures of democracy, but as successes stemming from trust and boredom. A functioning local democracy can build schools, plow roads, found libraries, and deliver public goods and services to its residents — this is important, but boring — and ultimately, it might be the best we can do when it comes to collective decision making.
