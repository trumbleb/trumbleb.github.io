Research & background

Dissertation & research focus

My dissertation, "After Much Discussion": Participation, Trust, and Boredom in the New England Town Meeting, examines the institution of New England Town Meeting — a form of direct, participatory, deliberative, actually existing local democracy. Famously valorized by Alexis de Tocqueville, depicted by Norman Rockwell in "Freedom of Speech," and appropriated as "Town Hall Meetings" in the 1990s, town meeting as it actually exists is far from the ideal type. It is not that town meeting is highly contested and anarchic, but the opposite — issues are largely decided through deliberation, consensus, and even disengaged apathy. I examine town meeting through a self-created longitudinal dataset of over 8,000 warrant articles (1956–2025) from over 200 meetings. In addition, I draw on ethnographic observation of 20 town meetings (2025–26), which I was able to observe through funded fieldwork. My dissertation asks us to reconsider low rates of participation not as failures of democracy, but as successes stemming from trust and boredom. A functioning local democracy can build schools, plow roads, found libraries, and deliver public goods and services to its residents — this is important, but boring — and ultimately, it might be the best we can do when it comes to collective decision making.
